# Kansas Early Vote 2026: Party, County, History

Registered Democrats led Kansas’s early vote by D+5.2 eight days before Election Day 2026; Republicans led at the same point in 2022 (R+3.4) and 2024 (R+14.7).

Published by [Carmena’s Overton & Electoral Research](https://carmenaresearch.com/), an independent, non-partisan US election research firm. These are the public tables behind our charts, from the article [Kansas early voters went from R+3 in 2022 to D+5 this year](https://carmenaresearch.com/blog/kansas-early-voters-r3-to-d5/). Party means voter registration, not vote choice, unless a column says otherwise. Figures are compared at the same number of days before Election Day in each year.

## Provenance and method
Carmena’s Overton compiles cumulative early-ballot counts from state and county election records and joins them to voter registration and vote history. Calculations are ours; the method is described on our [methodology page](https://carmenaresearch.com/methodology/). Last updated 2026-10-26. Updated as our tracking runs, typically weekly during the early-voting period.

## Caveats
- Party is voter registration, not vote choice. The 2026 comparison extract excludes Elk, Greeley and Wabaunsee (102 counties); the original source includes all 105. Earlier statewide totals cover all counties. Margin = (Democratic ballots − Republican ballots) ÷ all early ballots × 100.
- Dots and rings measure early voters’ registered parties at eight days out, not candidate choices. Filled dots are 2026; rings are 2022. Rest of Kansas combines the same 98 covered counties in both years, excluding Johnson, Sedgwick, Shawnee and Riley. Its margins are calculated from summed party counts, not an average of county margins; it does not imply every remaining county leans Republican. The aggregate row is not an additional county. Share bars compare explicitly labeled populations: all 2024 ballots versus covered 2026 early ballots. The 2026 statewide extract covers 102 counties; historical statewide totals cover all 105. Weight inputs and denominators are retained on the Johnson row in the download.
- Groups count how many of the four preceding general elections each early voter cast a ballot in, from each cycle’s matched voter file: 2014–2020 for 2022 and 2018–2024 for 2026. “None of the last four” includes newly eligible voters and people new to Kansas; it is not a count of lifetime first-time voters. Totals cover 201,731 early ballots in 2026 and 134,769 in 2022 across 103 counties, within about 1% of the statewide counts used elsewhere in the article. Margins are registered parties, not candidate choices.
- Both years use the same 102 covered counties. Compiled by Carmena’s Overton from state and county early-vote records and voter registration; calculations are ours. Turnout = early ballots ÷ registered voters × 100. Some small counties had barely reported at the 2022 comparison point. Underlying voter files are not reproduced; aggregate denominators are in the table.
- Early ballots are advance ballots cast in person plus mail ballots returned, including mail accepted after Election Day. 2016–2020 figures are the Secretary of State’s; 2022 and 2024 early counts come from the tracker’s voter-file series, divided by the Secretary of State’s certified ballot totals. For 2022 the tracker records 136,204 returned mail ballots; the Secretary of State’s county file totals 129,973 but lists 4 counties as N/A. The 2026 count is unfinished and covers 102 counties, so no 2026 share is shown.
- All solid lines measure early voters’ registered parties. Dotted segment: current 2026 D-minus-R margin plus the observed 2022 change from eight days out to day zero. Exact inputs: 2026 89,923 D and 79,331 R out of 203,818; 2022 at eight days out 57,281 D and 61,937 R out of 135,670; completed 2022 early count 145,079 D and 190,662 R out of 387,668. Only the hypothetical endpoint is calculated; the straight connecting segment is not a daily projection. This is not a forecast of turnout or candidate votes. Current coverage is 102 counties, historical statewide totals cover 105.
- The first row counts Johnson County residents recorded as voting in the 2024 general election by any method, including Election Day, by the party in the April 2, 2025 voter file; it is not a reconstructed election-day affiliation. The file records 345,069 voters, 99.0% of the county’s 348,594 certified ballots. Party margins use all recorded voters; the presidential margin uses votes for all candidates. Unaffiliated voters and cross-party support both contribute to the gap; totals cannot identify individual crossover voters.
